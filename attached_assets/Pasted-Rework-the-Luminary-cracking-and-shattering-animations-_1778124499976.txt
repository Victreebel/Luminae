Goal:
Replace the current crack/shatter implementation with a cleaner version that feels like a sealed crystalline vessel fracturing under pressure.

Sound timing to preserve:
- First Crack.mp3 = first major crack appears
- Second Crack.mp3 = second major crack appears
- Deep Impact.mp3 = moment shards/chunks first burst apart
- Glass Shatter.mp3 = main physical rupture / shard separation
- Cosmic Portal Boom.mp3 = flash resolves into portal/entity reveal

Cracking design:
1. Start from scratch.
2. The first crack should appear as a thin sharp white physical fracture.
3. After the white crack appears, a colored glow should chase along the crack path using the Luminary’s primary affinity/theme color.
4. Do not use a generic “increasing glow everywhere” effect.
5. Keep the white fracture line visible as the core, with the color moving/chasing along it and leaving residual colored energy behind it.
6. The second major crack should use the same pattern: thin white fracture first, then colored glow chasing along it.
7. Favor more branching rather than long stray secondary lines.
8. The smaller fracture lines were too long in the last iteration. Make them shorter.
9. Each branch should have at least one angle/kink so it feels like real fracture branching, not straight decorative scratches.
10. Branches should be thinner than the main cracks.
11. Crack ends/tips should be pointed/tapered, not flat or blunt.
12. Keep the crack network readable and not too webby or cluttered.
13. Cracks must act as the blueprint for the shatter pattern.

Shatter design:
1. Use irregular polygonal shard/chunk shapes, not square or rectangular pieces.
2. The crack boundaries should clearly define the future chunk boundaries.
3. When Deep Impact.mp3 hits, the chunks should first break free.
4. When Glass Shatter.mp3 hits, the chunks should visibly separate and rupture.
5. After they burst apart, the pieces should not hover awkwardly.
6. The pieces should separate, rotate in 3D, then fall/drift downward and fade out.
7. Favor this motion language:
   burst apart → slow slightly → fall/drift downward → fade into light
8. Avoid projectile motion toward the player.
9. Avoid pieces lingering too long.
10. Keep them physical and readable, but let them fade as they fall.
11. Maintain themed light underneath the broken panel using the Luminary’s theme/affinity color(s), not plain white unless appropriate.
12. Do not show a duplicate intact panel underneath.

Visual style notes:
- The panel is a sealed vessel under pressure.
- Cracks should feel physical first, magical second.
- The colored glow should feel like energy traveling through damage.
- The shatter should feel like real broken material losing support and being consumed by light.

Keep everything else the same unless necessary to support this rewrite.
