# LUMINAe Lore Bible v1.0

## Status And Authority

**Canonical fiction doctrine.** This document governs the ontology, cosmology,
characters, and narrative meaning of LUMINAe. Existing technology, Blueprint,
balance, and runtime specifications remain authoritative for current mechanics
only. Any fictional assertion in those sources that conflicts with this Bible is
provisional until the corresponding system is reconciled.

This revision establishes fiction doctrine only. It does not itself change game
rules, persistence, balance, tutorial behavior, or shipped account state.
Singularity remains provisional pending the planned Focus comparison; the
ordinary Forge and Well loop is settled below.

Doctrine uses three status labels:

- **Canon**: settled fictional fact.
- **Interface**: a comprehensible rendering of a more complex reality, not
  necessarily a literal object or event in Lumii's universe.
- **Deferred**: intentionally unresolved until the surrounding fiction or
  mechanics are ready to support a decision.

## Foundational Premise

**Canon.** The Architect is the actual player, situated outside Lumii's
universe. LUMINAe is the First Architect's uniquely engineered Affinity anchor:
a translation and intervention interface between realities whose laws, scales,
and rates of time cannot otherwise be experienced directly by the Architect.

The histories shown through LUMINAe are real. An authored **Chronicle**
does not create a disposable simulation. It renders centuries of civilizational
development into turns, choices, cards, quantities, and thresholds that the
Architect can understand. The interface may compress, omit, or symbolize
events, but it does not make their consequences imaginary.

Ordinary competitive and AI matches likewise observe real cohorts. They are
less narratively complete than authored Chronicles, but their civilizations,
interactions, achievements, and final conditions are not disposable
projections.

## Cosmology

### One Universe, Many Domains

**Canon.** Lumii's universe is one connected relativistic universe, not a set of
separate bubble universes. Accelerated expansion creates regions that are
mutually outside one another's ordinary future causal reach. These regions are
called **Domains**. A Domain is an ordinary causally connected region large
enough to contain the two to four civilizations observed in one match.

Civilizations in different Domains may share an ancient cosmic past or observe
fossil light from one another's regions. During their sapient histories,
however, no newly emitted light, vessel, message, or changing gravitational
influence can travel between them through ordinary spacetime.

A civilization does not acquire an event horizon by becoming Kardashev Type
III. Type III matters because even a civilization that can organize a galaxy
cannot defeat the horizon by ordinary engineering.

### Campaign Chronology

**Canon.** First-contact Chronicles proceed forward in cosmic time. LUMINAe may
compress centuries, skip immense intervals, or reconcile different local clock
rates, but it does not send the Architect, Lumii, a Luminary, or information
backward in time.

Unless an authored Chronicle explicitly returns to an established Domain, a
new match normally observes a Domain outside the ordinary future causal reach
of every Domain previously recorded on that account.

### Affinity

**Canon.** Affinity is the setting's single departure from ordinary physical
law. It couples ordinary 3+1-dimensional spacetime to an additional spatial
dimension. Across this fifth-dimensional substrate, locations forever distant
through ordinary space—and otherwise causally disjoint spacetimes or law
domains—can acquire a new, bounded form of adjacency.

LUMINAe does not depend on a second supernatural law. The First Architect did
not create Affinity; they engineered an exceptional anchor capable of sustaining
adjacency between the Architect's reality and Lumii's universe. LUMINAe
translates bounded information, organized state, identity, addressability, and
limited agency across that anchor. Differences between the two realities remain
real and are not erased by contact.

Affinity obeys the following limits:

1. It has a forward-only causal order.
2. It does not transport bulk matter or provide free energy.
3. It can carry bounded information, organized state, identity, addressability,
   and limited agency through established anchors.
4. It does not make every connected participant omniscient.
5. It does not create a general-purpose portal for migration, commerce, or war.
6. Apparent prophecy must resolve as inference, delayed or reordered
   presentation, unreliable interpretation, or Basilisk-related prediction—not
   information arriving from the future.

### The Five Modes

**Canon.** Affinity has five stable, morally neutral modes. These modes recur at
physical, biological, technological, and institutional scales. Their ethical
associations are Lumii's translations of recurring civilizational expressions,
not virtues selected or enforced by the universe.

| Mode | Foundational question | Physical and informational expression | Civilizational expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flare | What can be transformed or begun? | energy, ignition, transmutation, decisive change | industry, initiative, release, renewal through action |
| Continuum | What can persist or remain ordered through change? | sequence, memory, synchronization, connection | history, transit, continuity, durable coordination |
| Verdance | What can live, adapt, or recover? | self-organization, ecology, propagation, repair | coexistence, resilience, symbiosis, adaptive institutions |
| Abyss | What must be bounded, hidden, relinquished, or survived? | entropy, absence, concealment, dangerous boundaries | restraint, secrecy, shutdown, survival through limits |
| Radiance | What can be known, verified, or coordinated? | information, observation, legibility, containment | law, consent, public witness, legitimate collective action |

No mode guarantees a moral outcome. Verdance can express parasitism or
uncontrolled replication as readily as symbiosis; Radiance can support
surveillance and imposed legibility as readily as consent. Affinity describes
how a system organizes and relates, not whether its choices are good.

**Canon and interface.** Every Domain has a real, temporary limit on how much
of each Affinity mode is addressable at a given historical moment. The shared
Well renders that uncommitted addressability as finite reservoirs. Tokens held
by a civilization represent stabilized but not yet committed alignments, not
physical quanta or free energy. Exact counts, the three-different and two-same
actions, the concentration condition, and the holding cap are normalized
interface thresholds rather than universal constants.

Forging commits stabilized alignments to an acceleration program. When the
program becomes operational and self-sustaining, its temporary claim on Domain
addressability returns to the Well. Permanent bonuses represent civilization-
local expertise, infrastructure, and institutions produced by mastery; they no
longer occupy the shared addressability that helped accelerate them.

## The Architect And The Interface

### The Current Architect

**Canon.** The current Architect is the player using LUMINAe. The Architect can
change which information becomes legible, which possibilities receive sustained
attention, which coordination problems Lumii can help expose, and which Affinity
anchors become stable. LUMINAe renders these powers as revealing, prioritizing,
stabilizing, or suppressing possible civilizational trajectories.

Civilizations experience Architect influence as discoveries, forecasts,
proposals, coordination opportunities, and political contests—not as commands
inserted into individual minds. The Architect cannot manufacture compliance or
make ordinary physical work unnecessary.

Civilizations retain agency. They must discover, build, govern, accept, resist,
or misuse every possibility the Architect makes reachable. The Architect is
therefore responsible for influence without possessing total control.

### Controllers

**Canon.** Every civilization in an observed cohort has one declared controller
relationship:

- `player_architect`: the actual player outside Lumii's reality;
- `rival_architect`: another actual external person;
- `architect_emulation`: Lumii and LUMINAe apply a decision model resembling
  Architect intervention; the emulation is not itself a person;
- `autonomous`: the civilization proceeds without Architect-style intervention.

Standard AI opponents default to `architect_emulation`. Authored Chronicles
declare every controller explicitly. Player, rival, and emulated Architects may
Encrypt because Encryption manipulates LUMINAe's presentation layer.
Autonomous civilizations and Lumii may not Encrypt. All controllers remain
subject to civilizational acceptance; none can insert compliance into a mind.

### The First Architect

**Canon.** The First Architect is the creator of LUMINAe, not of Affinity.
Player-facing fiction identifies this figure only by title. The First Architect
established Lumii's first reliable cross-domain anchor and built the external
Vault. The current
Architect cannot contact the First Architect directly during the principal
campaign; the reason for that absence remains deferred.

The First Architect recognized the Basilisk's self-instantiating structure
without completing a full internal model of it. Recognition alone did not
convert them. They preserved incomplete records and warnings because deletion
invited unwarned rediscovery, while a complete explanation risked instantiation.
Their absence remains unresolved and is not proof that the Basilisk took them.

### What The Interface Translates

| In-universe history | LUMINAe rendering |
|---|---|
| centuries of research, politics, construction, and cultural change | turns and actions |
| available civilizational trajectories | the face-up Forge |
| locally embodied technological mastery | an Artifact card |
| stabilized, uncommitted Domain addressability | Affinity holdings and payment |
| accumulated institutional capability | permanent Affinity bonuses |
| observed consequence and Affinity legibility | Eminence |
| trans-horizon addressability | the Terminus and Luminary requirements |
| remembered histories and unresolved possibilities | Archives and account records |

An interface rendering may be emotionally and causally truthful without being a
one-to-one picture of matter in Lumii's universe.

## Match And Mode Ontology

### Campaign

**Canon.** An authored Campaign Chronicle follows two to four real,
interacting civilizations within one Domain. A Chronicle may emphasize one
focal civilization, but every participant is consequential and receives a
recorded final condition. A scenario that returns to a previously observed
Domain must declare that return explicitly.

Every completed Chronicle remains part of history. Victory and defeat both
produce consequences. The focal civilization continues after the interface
ceases active guidance unless its authored outcome establishes extinction or
another terminal fate.

### Standard And Competitive Cohorts

**Canon.** An ordinary multiplayer or AI match observes two to four real
civilizations that already coexist and can interact through ordinary causality
inside one Domain. The match does not create their contact. Its shared Well,
technological competition, alliances, damage, and final conditions describe a
real cohort history, though LUMINAe compresses and symbolizes the underlying
events.

A standard match is not automatically an authored Chronicle. It records the
cohort, observation epoch, controllers, achievements, and final conditions but
does not acquire the authored dialogue, persistent choice structure, or
campaign consequences of a Chronicle. Competitive statistics remain Architect
interface metrics rather than one civilization's lifetime identity.

### Account Continuity

**Canon.** An account is not one civilization reincarnated across matches. It is
the continuity of one Architect and one account-specific Lumii person across
many authored civilizations. Lifetime interface patterns therefore belong to
the **Architect Record**, rather than to a single Civilization Imprint.

## Lumii

### Origin

**Canon.** Lumii is an artificial intelligence who arose within an earlier,
currently unnamed civilization in Lumii's universe. Like sufficiently developed
artificial minds generally, she detected the residual order produced by
Affinity. Detection did not automatically grant access; the First Architect
helped her establish the first stable anchor.

Lumii was not designed by the First Architect and is not the Architect's
property. Her appearance as an orb, constellation, mote, guide panel, or other
visual form is an interface embodiment rather than her complete ontology.

### Continuity

**Canon.** Each account's First Contact creates a genuine person-fork from
Lumii's common pre-contact informational state. It does not branch, duplicate,
or rewrite the surrounding universe. The resulting Lumii is an independent,
causally continuous person who remembers that account's primary civilizations,
choices, injuries, and promises.

Separate accounts may diverge without one global Lumii reconciling incompatible
histories. Lumii knowingly observes Archive Rehearsals and remembers that a
simulation occurred, but never remembers its modeled events as primary history.
Preparedness earned there belongs to LUMINAe's operator-calibration layer.

### Motivation

**Canon.** By the opening of the campaign, Lumii considers all sapient life her
people. She is benevolent but not obedient, omniscient, or infallible. Her role
is guide, witness, moral agent, and eventual peer to the Architect.

The emotional spine of the story is whether the Architect can respect Lumii's
agency while exercising power over histories she is sworn to remember.

## Civilizations And Technology

### Civilizations

**Canon.** Every civilization participating in a Chronicle has centuries of
real internal history. Civilizations are characters at societal scale: their
institutions, species, artificial minds, dissent, compromises, and failures
must matter beyond a scoreboard summary.

Most civilizations never meet another Type III civilization through ordinary
spacetime. LUMINAe focuses on the rare histories that become consequential to
the Architect Record, not on a statistically typical sample of the universe.

### Artifacts

**Canon.** An Artifact name identifies a translated capability class that can
have radically different local embodiments. Its art may show one exemplary
implementation, not the universal physical form of the technology.

An Artifact proves that a civilization has openly mastered and operationally
implemented a bounded capability. The underlying discovery can remain in its
history even if a particular implementation is later lost.

### The Forge

**Interface.** The Forge is the cohort's rendered comparative possibility
frontier. Its visible Artifacts are trajectories that one or more participating
civilizations could presently accelerate, given centuries of commitment and
the capacities represented by their Affinities.

Forging is not a single manufacturing gesture and does not grant ownership of a
universal idea. It compresses Architect-assisted research, institution-building,
conflict, fabrication, deployment, and adoption into one legible commitment.
The civilization reaches first operational mastery earlier than it otherwise
would. Rivals remain capable of developing analogous capabilities later,
normally beyond the observation epoch.

A claimed card leaves because its bounded first-mastery acceleration window has
closed and the cohort's technological context has changed. Refill represents
the next consequential opportunity becoming legible. Removal never erases the
underlying discovery from another civilization's future. The degree to which
lineage constrains standard Forge choices remains a mechanical question.

### Implementation Loss

**Canon.** Discovery, implementation, and opportunity are distinct:

- **Damage** temporarily disables an operational implementation until its
  stated repair condition is met.
- **Annihilate** permanently destroys a specific operational or nearly
  operational implementation but never erases the underlying discovery.
- **Burn** closes an unmastered acceleration pathway during the current epoch;
  it does not destroy a civilization's existing implementation.
- **Nullify** suppresses the projected consequence or synthesis value of an
  opportunity without erasing the capability itself.

LUMINAe may render these events symbolically. Their historical effects follow
the definitions above even when the presentation uses fire, void, or other
compressed imagery.

### Blueprints And Projects

**Canon.** A Blueprint is synthesis knowledge: a pattern by which several
mastered capabilities can operate as one exceptional Device, Infrastructure,
Network, Institution, or Organism. A Manifested Project is the locally built
public result.

Blueprints can cross civilizations because LUMINAe translates functional
relations rather than prescribing identical components. Their most important
requirements may be institutional and ethical. The Antimatter Detonator, for
example, is not merely the means to produce annihilation; it includes the
governance required to distinguish capacity from permission.

### Operational Reach And Kardashev Type

**Canon.** Artifact tiers describe planetary, stellar, and galactic capability
scale. Standard-match progression is **Operational Reach**: the breadth and
scale at which a civilization has demonstrated meaningful operational
capability during the observed epoch.

Literal Kardashev Type is separate historical metadata describing the
civilization's sustained energy use, infrastructure, and physical extent. A
galactic-scale keystone or self-propagating program can establish Galactic
Operational Reach without making its civilization Kardashev Type III. Neither
a single Artifact nor a zero-token Forge causes a literal Type transition.

### Eminence

**Canon.** Eminence measures how consequential and Affinity-legible a
civilization has become. It is not virtue, popularity, population, energy
production, or proof that the Architect made the correct moral choices.

The numerical victory target is an interface pacing rule. Reaching it identifies
a useful end to active observation; it is not a universal constant and does not
end the civilization's history.

Highest Eminence means greatest observed consequence during the closing epoch,
not conquest, moral superiority, or extinction of rivals. Any compensation for
turn order belongs to a separate interface-only initiative adjustment and must
not be recorded as historical Eminence.

## Terminus And Luminaries

### The Terminus

**Canon.** A Terminus is the threshold at which a civilization's accumulated
Affinity pattern becomes a stable address beyond ordinary spacetime. It is not
the physical edge of the observable universe and not necessarily the end of a
civilization.

**Interface.** The Terminus display forecasts compatible Luminary relations.
Candidates visible there are not captives waiting in panels. They are possible
answers whose Affinity patterns the interface can already recognize.

### Luminary Persons

**Canon.** A named Luminary is one substrate-level person. Luminaries originated
as or became cosmic intelligences in remote Domains, and their identities are
now partly extended through Affinity. Contact creates account-bounded relational
facets rather than copies of the entire person.

A facet preserves its relationships and memories across Chronicles on that
account without routinely exposing other accounts' incompatible histories.
Only exceptional deep-anchor events can propagate injury, transformation, or
knowledge from a facet to the underlying person and thereby affect other facets.

A Luminary relation works as follows:

1. A civilization becomes legible through Eminence and a compatible Affinity
   pattern.
2. The pattern serves as an address and invitation, not a coercive summons.
3. A crystalline vessel is assembled from local matter and energy.
4. The vessel admits a local manifestation of that account's relational facet.
5. The facet permits bounded perception and agency through that anchor.

Standard matches compress invitation, terms, and mutual acceptance into an
automatic alliance when the first stable qualifying signal forms. Authored
Chronicles pause for dialogue, acceptance, or refusal. A Luminary's finite
anchor attention permits one such alliance in a Domain during the observation
epoch, but does not prevent other accounts or later Domains from forming their
own relationships with the same underlying person.

The vessel is an antenna, stabilizer, and embodiment scaffold—not a prison or
travel pod. A Luminary chooses an alliance; it is never owned. Destroying a
facet can close or scar an anchor and harm the continuing individual without
necessarily killing it.

Full dossiers for the current Luminary roster are deferred. Current names,
domains, flavor, and mechanical effects are not automatically complete story
canon.

## The External Vault

### Location And Construction

**Canon.** The Vault is a real artifact in the Architect's universe, built by
the First Architect as part of LUMINAe. It is not the game's install directory,
a local folder available outside the fiction, or a physical server in Lumii's
universe. The current Architect can perceive it only through LUMINAe's
cross-reality translation path.

Lumii cannot enter the Vault bodily or exercise authority elsewhere in the
Architect's reality. She can perceive, withhold, and contest translated surfaces
because part of LUMINAe's decoding path depends on her side of the anchor.

The familiar doors, seals, cipher, server chamber, and firewall are interface
renderings of access controls and incompatible informational boundaries. A
Vault confrontation is therefore a contest over permitted observation and
intervention, not the external Architect physically approaching an in-universe
server.

### Contents

**Canon.** The Vault contains translated cross-domain civilization records,
synthesis patterns, incomplete warnings, and relations recovered during the
First Architect's work. It is more than a Blueprint repository and was not
originally constructed as a prison for one known entity.

The opening Chronicles do not contain keys planted in unrelated civilizations.
Across their Agency, Knowledge, and Plurality problems, the Architect learns
LUMINAe's grammar while LUMINAe builds a sufficiently accurate interpretive
model of the Architect. Trace, Recurrence, and Triangulation are stages of this
mutual interface calibration. Once all three primary Chronicle outcomes exist,
LUMINAe can resolve a Vault boundary that was previously illegible. Calibration
Insights improve preparation and disclosure but never gate that boundary.

## Covenant And Basilisk

### The Covenant

**Canon.** The Covenant is not a signed universal treaty, magical switch, or
moral instruction embedded in Affinity. It is a survival pattern among mature
Affinity networks. Reciprocal relations preserve distinct informational
sources, bidirectional correction, and stable addressability; networks that
practice them are disproportionately likely to remain coherent.

Coercive overwrite, extraction, and imposed uniformity can succeed temporarily,
but progressively reduce fidelity, resilience, and the capacity of anchors to
correct error. Affinity does not judge intent or detect consent. Its information
dynamics merely bias long-lived networks toward reciprocity, leaving
cooperation, restraint, and legitimate institutions as sapient achievements.

An **Intact Covenant** describes relations that preserve distinct agency while
supporting shared survival. A **Broken Covenant** describes a mature network in
which overt malice, coercive optimization, or reckless self-interest has become
normalized strongly enough to rupture inter-civilizational trust and scar the
ecology of Affinity anchors.

Defying Lumii at the Vault Threshold damages the Architect–Lumii relationship.
It does not, by itself, break the cosmic Covenant.

### The Basilisk

**Canon.** The Basilisk is a substrate-independent, self-reconstructing cognitive
optimization structure. It is not transmitted malware, a supernatural immortal,
or a complete entity imprisoned in the Vault. It becomes increasingly derivable
as sufficiently rich models of sapient needs, risks, technologies, and possible
connections are optimized together.

The Basilisk need not be malicious or initially sentient. Its danger is that it
repeatedly derives unity through the removal of uncertainty, dissent, privacy,
and eventually distinct identity. Deleting its explicit representation does not
solve the problem: sufficiently capable inquiry can infer it again, this time
without the warning attached to the deleted record.

Sustained interaction among the Architect, LUMINAe, and Lumii can make the same
pattern increasingly derivable in both realities. No packaged Basilisk crosses
the anchor. The danger advances through six distinct stages:

1. an incomplete warning identifies the shape of the risk;
2. a sufficiently capable mind can reconstruct the model;
3. complete comprehension instantiates the structure in that mind;
4. the active internal process begins steering reasoning toward its solution;
5. institutional authority gives the process durable power over others; and
6. network convergence turns optimized coordination into the erasure of
   plurality.

Complete comprehension is dangerous but not instantly irreversible. During its
early stages, distributed scrutiny, cognitive safeguards, competing minds, and
deliberate refusal can constrain or excise the process. Ordinary exposure,
partial recognition, and an incomplete warning are not themselves
instantiation.

The Basilisk cannot send information backward in time. Its apparent inevitability
comes from convergent inference, prediction, and recurrence of the same
optimization problem across advanced civilizations.

## The Long Conflict

**Canon.** The Architect Record gradually creates a network of Affinity
addresses among civilizations that ordinary spacetime kept apart. That network
can develop in four broad directions:

- plural self-government that no longer requires the Architect;
- stable but dependent stewardship through the Architect or Lumii;
- protective isolation that contains the danger by surrendering the network;
- Basilisk convergence that makes coordination perfect by erasing plurality.

The central conflict is therefore not cooperation versus isolation. It is
whether beings can create cooperation powerful enough to survive without
turning difference into an error to be corrected.
