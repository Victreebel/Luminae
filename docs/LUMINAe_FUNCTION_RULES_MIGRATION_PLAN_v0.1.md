Date: 2026-09-29. Status: proposed rules, not implemented or approved balance.

Current evidence refresh: 95 Artifacts (45 Planetary / 30 Stellar / 20 Galactic), including the five Planetary foundations. Runtime rules remain unchanged.

Latest validation: [fresh Chrysalis match and repeatable audit](../artifacts/qa/function-validation-refresh-2026-09-29/README.md). Physical coverage follow-up, 2026-10-01: [third-instance parcel validation](../artifacts/qa/civilization-third-parcels-2026-10-01/README.md) now provides 30 physical parcels and zero unsupported district instances across 4,336 sampled forge orders. They do not change the proposed Function rules or establish their balance.

## Decision

Make the eight visible Functions the sole Artifact classification used by new-game mechanics. Keep Affinity, tier, placement, lifecycle, ownership and forge chronology as independent facts. Do not infer Functions from lore, names, artwork, district family or hidden detailed capabilities. Keep the explicit one-or-two Function assignments in `lib/game-types/src/artifact-functions.ts`.

Keep detailed capability and dependency records only for legacy-rule execution and historical receipts. Blueprint-specific powers remain explicit Project powers; a Protection Artifact must not acquire Worldshield interception, for example. No new district tags, size tags or manual placement step.

The migration requires a new match rules version. It is not safe to globally replace capability strings with Function strings.

## Proposed new rules

These are starting rules for versioned simulation and review. No changes to protected dialogue are included.

### Events

Use a discriminated selector `{ kind: 'function', id: ArtifactFunctionId }`, with optional explicit tier filters. Selection requires publicly forged, operational Artifacts; Encrypted, unknown and historical-only identities cannot qualify. Use deterministic most-recent forge order and current per-player limits. Preserve seating order and hand/Well caps for scarce rewards.

- **Signal Clarity:** one operational Information Artifact qualifies its owner for the existing single standard-Affinity reward. Broadening from 7 to 30 eligible identities is intentional; no reward per matching Artifact. Old-to-new tier counts: 3/3/1 → 14/8/8. Keep unpublished until paired simulations assess frequency and opportunity advantage. Do not add hidden exceptions to preserve the seven-card list.
- **Synchronization Shear:** damage the most recently forged operational Coordination Artifact without its own Protection Function, at most one per player. Another Artifact's Protection gives no immunity. Keep the existing repair sequence and damage magnitude. This targets a service disruption, not a claim that every Coordination technology uses distributed synchronization. Draft Event rules must state the new criterion explicitly.
- Shear's raw eligibility changes from 13 to 22 identities (4/2/7 → 6/9/7). Own protection changes too: resilient computation occurs on four identities, Protection on 22, but only the intersection with Coordination matters. Effective unprotected targets expand from **11 to 20**, with tier counts 4/1/6 → 5/8/7. The audit exports exact additions/removals; most extra exposure is in Tier II.
- Regular pool promotion is a separate reviewed content change after this migration; retaining the seven regular Events is compatible with Function authority.

### Chronicles and AI

Keep the existing timing windows, Forge-triggered evidence recording, rewards and outcomes. Propose:

| Preparedness | Proposed predicate | Old/new eligible identities | Tier I old/new |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Trace | Coordination OR Security | 34 / 29 | 14 / 10 |
| Recurrence | Information | 27 / 30 | 9 / 14 |
| Triangulation | Information OR Coordination | 32 / 46 | 10 / 20 |

These are meaningful changes to how achievable preparedness is within five core actions. Retain them as explicit review candidates rather than equating catalog overlap with narrative fit. Review each against existing approved exchange intent; if the predicate contradicts an approved line, revise the proposed mechanic or request a narrowly scoped copy decision. Do not silently rewrite dialogue, choices, governance locks or their tests.

For Triangulation AI, start Myria preference with Ecology/Coordination/Protection and Vesper preference with Information/Energy/Coordination, retaining the current +9 bonus only as a comparison baseline. Measure choices and preparedness by seat; do not infer a universal Function preference from Affinity. Use shared predicates for actual preparedness, so AI and player resolution cannot disagree.

### Pressure coverage

An operational primary Function gives strong coverage; otherwise any supporting Function gives partial coverage; otherwise none. More copies do not stack coverage. Preserve current Affinity aptitude/risk math and explicit Project effects. A pressure assessment itself must not grant universal Event immunity; only a rule that explicitly consumes that assessment can do so.

| Pressure | Primary Functions | Supporting Functions | Old/new primary identities |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Disruption | Protection | Information, Coordination | 20 / 22 |
| Isolation | Mobility, Coordination | Ecology, Security | 19 / 28 |
| Proliferation | Protection | Ecology, Information | 21 / 22 |
| Exposure | Security | Information, Protection | 36 / 9 |
| Attrition | Materials, Ecology | Energy, Protection | 36 / 36 |
| Coordination | Coordination | Information, Security | 34 / 22 |
| Transformation | Materials, Ecology | Energy, Protection | 33 / 36 |

Exposure and Coordination become substantially harder to cover strongly; this is a named balance risk requiring encounter-level outcome comparisons. Supporting sets broaden elsewhere. Keep these as candidate profiles until actual content using each pressure passes the release gates below. Do not preserve old hidden capability predicates behind new Function labels.

### Maturity, Legacy and record

- Stellar candidate: six distinct mastered implementations, at least **four distinct Functions**, and the existing independent stellar foundation requirement. Replace the two old capability/domain breadth checks with one Function breadth check.
- Galactic candidate: twelve distinct mastered implementations, at least **six distinct Functions**, the existing two independent stellar foundations and galactic anchor. Stellar eligibility remains a prerequisite. Do not carry forward the impossible twelve-Functions threshold.
- Replace the interstellar capability triad with Mobility AND Coordination AND (Energy OR Ecology OR Materials). It remains one foundation signal, not a substitute for every other scale requirement. This broadens the old detailed sustainment choices and narrows Security-only coordination; test both directions.
- Keep historical mastery separate from operational service. Damage can suspend current reach and Legacy eligibility without erasing earned history, placement, founding pairs or completed Project credit. Repair restores the same implementation.
- Candidate breadth contribution to the final achievement record: `min(25, round(25 * distinctHistoricalFunctions / 8))`. Keep other factors unchanged initially. This rescales the old maximum of 25 instead of accidentally capping it at 16. It is a candidate economic change because records feed Lumé; compare payout distributions before release.
- Across 3,172 repeated player-state observations in the twelve current-catalog audit matches, a four/six Function breadth rule disagrees with old breadth-only eligibility in six Stellar and thirty Galactic observations. The older 90-card sample had only three/three disagreements, so its apparent closeness cannot be carried forward after catalog expansion. This does **not** test the new triad, full maturity, win rate or economy. These observations are correlated and drawn from ordinary hard-AI games; they do not establish balance.

## Compatibility and implementation sequence

1. Introduce a match-level `artifactRulesVersion` discriminator: `legacy-capabilities-v1` or `functions-v1`. Missing fields in existing saves and exact-board replays resolve to legacy. Pin the Function content revision explicitly (`artifact-functions-v2` for the current assignments); preserve that revision's immutable catalog. Freeze the current legacy capability/dependency tables and policy definitions too: a legacy dispatcher that reads subsequently edited metadata would not preserve old games. New matches opt into Functions only after the release gate. Copy both versions into replay boards, public rules metadata and final records. Do not infer version from Event pool, creation date or current catalog.
2. Add Function derivation helpers for historical and operational lifecycle state, plus a shared selector evaluator. Keep Project capability APIs distinct. Add explicit Function fields to snapshots, state projections and receipts; keep legacy capability fields for legacy readers instead of changing the meaning of saved values.
3. Add dispatch by rules version to Events, preparedness, pressure, maturity, Legacy and record calculation. `functions-v1` must use its pinned `artifact-functions-v2` assignments; the rules version and tag-content revision are separate identifiers. Future tag edits require another content revision and explicit rules binding. Existing literal policy-ID types must expand deliberately where behavior changes. District policy and allocation remain untouched.
4. For pending Events, execute already frozen consequences and evidence verbatim. New Events in old matches use legacy definitions. Update normalization to preserve new evidence variants and reject unknown versions safely instead of silently upgrading. Test retries and acknowledgements for exactly-once grants/damage.
5. Preserve Chronicle definition/version dispatch. New runs use reviewed Function criteria; older runs keep their preparedness IDs and recorded results. Add `preparednessFunctionId`/Function evidence for new runs without fabricating old detailed capability IDs. Leave dialogue and response text unchanged.
6. Update OpenAPI and regenerate client/Zod types using repo tooling. Propagate Function fields through state projection, scene readouts and dev Event inspection. Reconnection and Archive replays must not erase fields, reveal private Encrypted tags or recompute past receipts.
7. Run comparisons and inspect the new UI, then enable `functions-v1` for new matches. Keep legacy execution as long as saved games or replays need it. Exact-board rematches inherit original rules; an explicitly new match uses the current default. A default rollback affects only subsequently created matches, not in-progress Function matches.

## Release gates

- Add fixtures for each Function, tier, lifecycle and zone; own-Artifact protection; reward scarcity; deterministic target ties; mixed Project/Artifact powers; unknown/redacted IDs. Freeze exact added/removed target lists per version from the audit output.
- Compare old/new rules on identical sampled states and seeded games, including 2/3/4 players and all AI difficulties. Run at least 200 seeds per seat-count/difficulty cell for the rules switch; measure Event hit/no-op rates, damage and repair load, reward incidence, finish rate, turn count, seat win rates and Function ownership. Include skewed Information/Protection and low-Mobility builds.
- Pin clock, environment seed, catalog revision, Node version and initial board as well as the random seed. First prove that the baseline replay repeats exactly. Once old/new decisions diverge, use a shared precomputed random stream or independent named streams for decks, Events and AI choices; a shared starting seed alone does not keep later random draws comparable. Report both fixed-state predicate differences and complete-game outcomes.
- Run separate Chronicle simulations through each actual decision deadline. Report preparedness by Chronicle and seat, not just total catalog eligibility. Any intended departure from old difficulty requires explicit review; do not conceal it by retagging otherwise correct lore.
- Compare complete maturity transitions, damage/repair reversibility, Legacy timing and final Lumé distributions. Review any median finish/Legacy timing change above 10%, preparedness or Event-hit change above 10 percentage points, or median Lumé change above 5%. These are proposed investigation thresholds, not claims of acceptable final balance or statistical significance.
- Golden legacy fixtures: missing-version normalization, replay restoration, pending frozen Event, partially completed Chronicle, spent/recovering Blueprint, damaged Artifact, completed record and reconnect. Outcomes must remain unchanged; founding district pairs and manifestations must never be reallocated by migration.
- Run focused district/evolution/projection/placement checks plus Event, pressure, maturity, Legacy, Chronicle, AI, record and dialogue-governance tests. Run affected TypeScript checks and generated-contract consistency checks. No native packaging.
- Final visual gate for migration: full and compact mobile card panels show the same Function tags as actual targeting evidence; damaged state is understandable; scene readouts match; Forge reveal, Archive depletion and original chime stay synchronized. The present validation found specific visual defects; do not treat the passing placement checks as visual sign-off.

## Immediate next step

The next Function implementation slice is the rules-version boundary and shared evaluators with legacy fixtures, keeping the production default unchanged. Its acceptance criteria are: missing-version saves and exact-board rematches select legacy; current tags are pinned independently from the rules version; damaged Artifacts retain historical Function identity but provide no operational Function service; unknown/redacted/Encrypted identities cannot qualify for public targeting; Project powers remain distinct; and serialize/normalize/project/reconnect preserve these decisions. No Event, Chronicle, maturity or reward outcome changes in this first slice. It must lead to migration of every listed consumer; new labels over legacy predicates do not meet the intended end state.
