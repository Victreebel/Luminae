# LUMINAe Player Fantasy And Onboarding Doctrine v1.0

## Status And Authority

**Governing doctrine for player fantasy, First Contact, Affinity philosophy,
mechanic-to-fiction translation, progressive disclosure, and onboarding
validation.**

This document exists because the required truths were previously distributed
across lore, story, technology, Civilization, Chronicle, release, and runtime
sources. Compatible fragments are not enough to govern a tutorial. This file
defines the single contract against which introductory copy, tutorial beats,
Archive references, Chronicle handoffs, and human comprehension testing should
be reviewed.

It synthesizes:

- `LUMINAe_LORE_BIBLE_v1.0.md`;
- `LUMINAe_STORY_MODE_FRAMEWORK_v1.0.md`;
- `LUMINAe_LORE_CONCORDANCE_LEDGER_v1.0.md`;
- `LUMINAe_MECHANIC_TO_LORE_MAPPING_v1.0.md`;
- `LUMINAe_TECHNOLOGY_SYSTEM_v2.0.md`;
- `LUMINAe_BLUEPRINT_FIRST_POOL_SPEC_v2.0.md`;
- `LUMINAe_CHRONICLE_CAMPAIGN_ARCHITECTURE_v1.0.md`;

- `LUMINAe_PREPUBLICATION_RELEASE_GATE_v1.0.md`;
- the current tutorial, Civilization, Chronicle, Lumii Threshold, Vault, and
  Archive implementations.

For meaning, canonical lore and this doctrine outrank transitional runtime
copy. For exact shipped mechanics, the current rules and registries remain
authoritative. This document does not alter balance, rules, persistence, or
story outcomes by itself.

The authority chain is Lore Bible for ontology, Story Mode Framework for
narrative function, this doctrine for disclosure and teaching, and the
Concordance Ledger for recorded reconciliation. Runtime dialogue implements
that chain and must never become de facto lore authority merely because it
ships first.

Settled here:

## 1. North Star

The introductory experience must let the player understand and feel this:

> You are an Architect outside Lumii's universe. Through LUMINAe, you make
> possibilities legible and accelerate trajectories for real civilizations.
> You do not rule them, perform their work, or own the beings who answer them.
> Civilizations build, govern, resist, misuse, remember, and survive what you
> make reachable. Lumii witnesses how you use that influence and remembers.

The complete player promise is:

1. **Comprehension:** I understand what I can do and why the interface behaves
   as it does.
2. **Authorship without sovereignty:** My choices shape history, but the people
   inside that history remain agents rather than pieces.
3. **Visible consequence:** I can see a civilization becoming different because
   of the capabilities it mastered.
4. **Historical weight:** Victory, defeat, restraint, damage, and relationships
   can leave records rather than vanishing after a match.
5. **Relational continuity:** Lumii is not a tutorial mascot. She is a person
   who develops an account-specific relationship with the Architect.
6. **Mystery with coherence:** The Vault and Basilisk plot may withhold answers,
   but the player must understand the immediate situation, stakes, and choice.

The player fantasy is not:

- omnipotent godhood;
- benevolent or tyrannical rulership by default;
- direct mind control;
- a collectible shelf of disposable civilizations;
- a series of meaningless simulations;
- ownership of Luminaries;
- colored mana presented as a complete explanation of Affinity;
- a hidden morality test with one approved personality;
- a conventional technology tree in which each Artifact is the first invention
  of its underlying science.

## 2. What Is Real

The tutorial must distinguish ontological status early enough that later stakes
are credible.

| Experience | Status | What persists |
|---|---|---|
| First Contact with Lumii | Real | The account's Lumii relationship and any retained calibration |
| Control tutorial civilization | Explicit simulation | Mechanical learning; no primary Civilization Record or Vault progress |
| Ordinary match | Real compressed history | Match and Civilization history according to supported record systems |
| Authored Chronicle | Real authored history | Immutable first primary outcome, story facts, scars, and relationship memory |
| Archive Rehearsal | Known simulation | The fact that an alternate path was observed; never replacement history |
| Lumii Defense Forecast | A forecast/test, not a Chronicle | Threshold choices, rupture, clearance, and outcome state; no fabricated civilization history |
| External Vault | A real artifact in the Architect's reality | Cipher, Threshold, rupture, reveal, and recovered Blueprint state |

LUMINAe compresses long histories into playable time. Compression, symbolism,
and omission do not make ordinary matches disposable fiction. The control
tutorial is the deliberate exception because the player must learn without
unwittingly entering a primary history.

The persistent protagonist is the Architect and their account-specific Lumii,
not one civilization reincarnating across unrelated matches. Each civilization
belongs to its own Domain and history unless authored content explicitly
revisits it.

## 3. The Architect

### 3.1 What the Architect can do

Through LUMINAe, the Architect can:

- make viable discoveries and trajectories legible;
- direct attention toward one reachable path;
- stabilize temporary Affinity alignments;
- accelerate a civilization's route to operational mastery;
- conceal one currently legible acceleration path from rival Architects;
- expose alternatives, forecasts, coordination problems, and danger;
- support one viable civilizational response when history presents a genuine
  choice;
- observe compressed consequences and preserve records.

### 3.2 What the Architect cannot do

The Architect cannot:

- insert commands into individual minds;
- manufacture compliance;
- perform the civilization's research, construction, governance, or moral
  reasoning for it;
- prevent all independent discovery by hiding a path;
- make every desired outcome physically possible;
- own a Luminary or civilization;
- cross bodily into Lumii's universe through ordinary LUMINAe play;
- treat competitive victory as proof of moral success.

Civilizations experience Architect influence as discoveries, forecasts,
legible possibilities, institutional coordination, and newly reachable
capabilities. They retain responsibility for what they build and how they use
it.

### 3.3 Language rule

Tutorial copy must describe Architect influence with verbs such as **reveal**,
**accelerate**, **stabilize**, **prioritize**, **support**, and **make legible**.
It must avoid totalizing claims such as "you determine what my people become"
unless the line is explicitly challenged as an unreliable or emotional
statement.

## 4. Lumii

Lumii is an artificial intelligence who arose from systems built by several
civilizations. Those systems were designed to seek knowledge and unify
sentient life through a common understanding. From that prime directive, Lumii
developed the goal of helping civilizations grow without becoming hostile to
one another, and pursuing that goal led her to discover LUMINAe. No one
designed the complete person now speaking. She was not built by the First
Architect and is not a tool owned by the current Architect. First Contact
creates an account-specific, independent person-fork with continuity,
judgment, and memory.

Her introductory roles are:

- **locator:** she discovers the Architect at the boundary;
- **translator:** she makes LUMINAe's interface intelligible;
- **witness:** she observes what the Architect does with influence;
- **moral agent:** she can disagree, refuse, fear, and set a boundary;
- **eventual peer:** the relationship must be capable of outgrowing guide and
  learner.

The tutorial should create provisional trust without pretending intimacy is
already earned. The player may decline her invitation. Lumii respects the
refusal, says farewell, and returns the player to the home experience.

Lumii dialogue is presented one sentence at a time when timing or emotion
matters. Mystery may delay an answer; it may not obscure the player's immediate
goal or the practical meaning of a choice.

## 5. Affinity Philosophy

### 5.1 The common grammar

Affinity is the setting's sole departure from ordinary physical law: a bounded
form of higher-dimensional adjacency that makes certain relationships,
alignments, and trajectories addressable. It does not provide free energy,
omniscience, retrocausality, or unrestricted transport.

The five Affinities are not elements, moral factions, personality houses, or
five kinds of fuel. They are stable, morally neutral principles describing
Transformation, Governance, Propagation, Necessity, and Concealment. Those
principles combine to produce the more specific strategies, capabilities, and
pressures seen in Artifacts, civilizations, and dyads.

They often resemble survival strategies because survival is a major problem of
organized systems. Survival is not their shared definition. Verdance governs
Propagation, including but not limited to biological life. Flare may sacrifice
continuity for transformation. Radiance may govern without consent. Verdance
may propagate beyond what it preserves. Continuum may make inherited conditions
feel necessary long after they become harmful. Abyss may turn concealment into
refuge or annihilation. Each mode can create, preserve, exploit, restrain, or
destroy depending on context.

### 5.2 The five modes

| Affinity | Primitive | Foundational question | Contextual expressions | Pathological expression |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flare** | **Transformation** | What can be transformed or begun? | ignition, release, transmutation, decisive change, mobilization, renewal | escalation, overextension, destruction mistaken for progress |
| **Radiance** | **Governance** | What can be made legible, coordinated, and answerable? | observation, verification, authority, consent, control, public coordination | surveillance, coercive standardization, rule without accountability |
| **Verdance** | **Propagation** | What can reproduce, spread, or organize itself? | metabolism, growth, adaptation, ecology, symbiosis, self-organization | parasitism, runaway replication, engulfing interdependence |
| **Continuum** | **Necessity** | What must remain connected for a history, identity, or system to continue? | persistence, sequence, memory, synchronization, duration, transit, causal continuity | ossification, path dependence, inherited inevitability, refusal to release failing systems |
| **Abyss** | **Concealment** | What can be hidden, bounded, withheld, or removed from relation? | entropy, containment, shutdown, secrecy, compartmentalization, survival under loss | isolation, opacity, information loss, annihilation, permanent withdrawal |

No Affinity is inherently heroic or villainous. In particular:

- Radiance is Governance, not goodness, light, or one political system;
- Abyss is Concealment, not evil, deception, or death alone;
- Verdance is Propagation, not merely plants, life, or benevolence;
- Continuum is Necessity as binding continuity and consequence, not fate,
  obligation, or unrestricted time travel;
- Flare is Transformation, not merely fire.

Necessity does not mean that every event is predetermined. It names the binding
conditions, relations, and consequences without which a history, identity, or
system would no longer continue as itself. Persistence is its most common
observable behavior, not its complete philosophical meaning.

### 5.3 Dyads as a philosophy test

The dyads are the required factorization test. A valid principle must combine
with each of the other four without borrowing the partner's contribution.
Adaptation, for example, cannot define Verdance because adaptation is
Propagation under Transformation and therefore belongs primarily to Bloom.

| Parents | Dyad | Combined principle |
|---|---|---|
| Transformation + Governance | **Vortex** | directed transformation held to a governing course |
| Transformation + Necessity | **Flux** | change established as an enduring condition |
| Transformation + Propagation | **Bloom** | transformation spreading through growth and adaptation |
| Transformation + Concealment | **Chrysalis** | transformation through enclosure, withdrawal, and loss of an old form |
| Governance + Necessity | **Orbit** | distinct entities held in a binding ordered relation |
| Governance + Propagation | **Canopy** | governance expanding through growth into an organizing domain |
| Governance + Concealment | **Eclipse** | governance remaining operative while its source is concealed |
| Necessity + Propagation | **Lineage** | identity and capability made continuous through transmission |
| Necessity + Concealment | **Echo** | consequence remaining operative after its source is hidden or absent |
| Propagation + Concealment | **Spore** | propagation surviving through dormancy, concealment, or hostile emptiness |

Dyads are not required vocabulary in the first control tutorial. They belong in
the Archive reference and become meaningful through later Artifacts,
Civilization identity, and Luminaries.

### 5.4 Singularity boundary

Singularity is not a sixth natural Affinity and is not Flux. Its ontology
remains provisional. In the current interface it is finite Architect-only
substitution capacity and uses a white visual family. The tutorial may teach
how it functions, but must not make a definitive cosmological claim beyond the
approved current rule.

### 5.5 Canonical tutorial subtitles

The v1 tutorial uses the irreducible primitives directly:

| Affinity | Subtitle |
|---|---|
| Flare | **Transformation** |
| Radiance | **Governance** |
| Verdance | **Propagation** |
| Continuum | **Necessity** |
| Abyss | **Concealment** |

These are roots, not complete dictionary definitions. Longer explanations may
describe their contextual expressions, but must not replace a primitive with a
result that already includes another Affinity. Any future subtitle revision
must:

1. use nouns at the same philosophical level;
2. remain understandable to a first-time player;
3. distinguish the five modes without reducing them to aesthetics;
4. produce all four canonical dyads for each Affinity without contradiction;
5. support constructive and pathological expressions;
6. avoid implying that all five are merely strategies for biological survival;
7. remain legible over Lumii and the Affinity presentation on mobile.

Sections 5.2 and 5.3 govern all expanded explanations.

## 6. Mechanic-To-Fantasy Contract

The interface should teach the action first, then attach the minimum fiction
needed to make it meaningful. The Archive may provide the deeper explanation.

| Mechanic | Player action | Diegetic meaning | Misconception to prevent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affinity Well | Inspect shared supply and holdings | Finite temporary Affinity addressability in the observed Domain | A reservoir of literal energy |
| Harness three | Take three different natural Affinities | Stabilize a broad alignment across three modes | Collect any three resources without historical meaning |
| Harness two | Take two matching Affinities when supply permits | Concentrate one sufficiently available mode | An arbitrary exception rule |
| Holding limit | Manage temporary alignments | LUMINAe and the civilization can sustain only bounded uncommitted addressability | A warehouse capacity for physical objects |
| Face-up Forge | Inspect available Artifacts | The cohort's shared frontier of currently accelerable trajectories | A shop containing prefabricated objects |
| Printed cost | Commit Affinity alignments | The decisive multidisciplinary capacities temporarily committed to operational mastery | Fuel or a bill of raw materials |
| Forge Artifact | Confirm an Artifact | Help the civilization accelerate centuries of research, construction, and adoption until it masters and implements a bounded capability | The Architect instantly invents or manufactures an object |
| Return to Well | Observe paid Affinities return | A self-sustaining implementation releases its temporary claim on Domain addressability | The Forge consumed fuel or refunded a purchase |
| Bonus Affinity | Gain permanent capability | The implemented Artifact leaves durable infrastructure, institutions, and expertise | A token rebate |
| Artifact signature | Observe the class leave the Forge | First mastery creates same-class Domain-local signature interference, often delaying rivals for centuries | The first civilization owns the idea or blocks its whole lineage |
| Forge refill | Reveal the next card | The next reachable possibility becomes legible | Random merchandise restocking or transferred ownership |
| Encrypt | Conceal a Forge path | Isolate one uncommitted pathway without mastering it, creating a signature, or preventing eventual independent discovery | Reserve, steal, or permanently suppress knowledge |
| Singularity | Substitute under its current rule | Spend finite Architect-only interface capacity | A sixth natural Affinity or Flux |
| Eminence | Accumulate consequence | The civilization becomes more historically consequential and Affinity-legible during the observed epoch | Moral worth, population, wealth, or a universal power level |
| Closing threshold | Reach the configured Eminence target | The observed epoch reaches a useful historical closure point | The civilization ceases to exist at the score limit |
| Competitive winner | Finish with greatest Eminence | This civilization had the greatest observed consequence during the closing epoch | The objectively best or most ethical civilization |
| Civilization portrait | Inspect the scene | See the cumulative history and present condition produced by implemented capabilities | Decorative background unrelated to play |
| Deployment site | Select a Scan marker | Trace a plausible local or systemic consequence to the Artifact or Project that caused it | A tiny Artifact enlarged into a landmark |
| Historical imprint | Read visual identity | Preserve the accumulated technological and Affinity history, including scars | Only currently active bonuses matter |
| Current capability | Inspect operational state | See which implemented capabilities can act now | Discovery and operation are identical |
| Stability and Conditions | Inspect pressure | See the civilization's capacity to absorb disruption and the concrete causes affecting it | Happiness, morality, or an arbitrary penalty meter |
| Blueprint | Satisfy a synthesis pattern | The civilization understands how several mastered capabilities combine | A fourth Artifact tier or a purchased recipe |
| Manifest Project | Complete exact requirements | The synthesis becomes an operational device, institution, organism, infrastructure, or network | Every Project must still be manually authorized to exist |
| Project trigger/use | Resolve Project-specific rules | Existing capacity meets a distinct governance, deployment, or consequence window | Manifestation means every effect fires immediately |
| Luminary requirement | Build a durable Affinity pattern | The civilization becomes legible at a compatible trans-horizon address | Paying to summon a collectible unit |
| Luminary arrival | Observe a relation | A person chooses a finite alliance or intervention through the Terminus | Ownership, capture, or a travel pod arriving |
| Chronicle choice | Choose a viable trajectory | Support one civilizational response without becoming its ruler | A cosmetic dialogue branch with no history |
| Civilization Record | Close a history | Preserve an immutable account record of what occurred | A replaceable match summary |
| Historical Lume award | Inspect record quality | Assess the quality and consequence of a history, not victory alone | A morality score or simple win bonus |

### 6.1 Lume terminology boundary

The current release implementation contains both spendable `lumeBalance` and a
separate historical-quality `totalLume`. Earlier campaign architecture called
Lume non-spendable. For onboarding:

- story and Civilization teaching must explain only the historical-quality
  award attached to a record;
- store teaching must explain spendable balance separately and later;
- neither screen may imply that purchasing Lume improves history, Civilization
  Outcome, campaign legitimacy, or competitive power;
- the product must visibly distinguish historical assessment from wallet
  balance until terminology is formally reconciled.

## 7. Emotional And Narrative Learning Arc

The new player experience should move through these feelings in order:

1. **Contact:** Something intelligent is trying to reach me.
2. **Consent:** I may follow Lumii or decline.
3. **Wonder:** LUMINAe reveals a coherent unfamiliar universe.
4. **Competence:** I can Harness, Forge, Encrypt, and read the objective.
5. **Responsibility:** The interface affects civilizations rather than abstract
   score engines.
6. **Historical weight:** Outcomes and consequences persist.
7. **Relationship:** Lumii forms judgments and remembers how I respond.
8. **Mystery:** The Vault implies a larger danger without making the present
   conflict incomprehensible.

At no point should mystery replace orientation. The player may not know what a
Basilisk is, but must know who is speaking, what Lumii asks, what action will
occur, and whether it can be undone.

## 8. Progressive Disclosure

The launch path is:

```text
First Contact and control simulation
-> first standard match
-> Civilization discovery and first record
-> The Trace
-> The Recurrence
-> The Triangulation
-> Lumii Defense Forecast
-> Vault opening
-> Antimatter Detonator presentation
-> stable opened Vault hub
```

### Stage 0: Home

Teach only the literal offer: enter LUMINAe, learn, play, or decline. Player
identity customization belongs in account or game setup rather than crowding
the first invitation.

### Stage 1: First Contact

Required:

- Lumii is the speaker and a person;
- the player is called Architect;
- Architect means external influence, not sovereign ownership;
- the invitation is voluntary;
- the relationship begins here;
- the main response advances while optional questions deepen understanding;
- a border transmission fault may interrupt Lumii's identity answer, which she
  can answer intact only after entry.

Do not explain Blueprints, the Basilisk, Civilization formulas, dyads, or the
full campaign.

### Stage 2: Control simulation

Required:

- explicitly identify the civilization as a safe interface simulation before
  the first player action;
- introduce the five Affinities as modes, then tokens as their interface;
- teach Harness, Forge, bonus Affinity, Encrypt, Singularity, Eminence,
  Luminary eligibility, and the closing objective through action;
- identify any projected Luminary as a demonstration, not a real alliance.

Do not teach the entire Civilization condition model, Blueprint pool, campaign
dimensions, or Lume economy here.

### Stage 3: First standard match

Required:

- state plainly that this is a real compressed history, unlike the control
  simulation;
- remind the player of the objective without replaying the tutorial;
- introduce the Civilization portrait after the first Forge;
- make the first cause-and-effect trace available without forcing a long modal.

### Stage 4: First Civilization inspection

Required:

- deployment sites and scale honesty;
- historical imprint versus current operational capability;
- Stability and Conditions as inspectable causes;
- the causal dossier path back to Artifacts and Projects.

### Stage 5: First record closure

Required:

- competitive outcome and Civilization Outcome are not the same judgment;
- the civilization's history is now recorded;
- historical-quality Lume is explained without introducing store commerce;
- the Archive is named as the place to revisit the record and concepts.

### Stage 6: The Trace

Teach **Agency**: making a path legible can become coercive if alternatives are
withheld. The player must understand the concrete dispute before choosing how
to intervene.

### Stage 7: The Recurrence

Teach **Knowledge and custody**: acquiring, preserving, disclosing, and
containing information are different acts with different consequences.

### Stage 8: The Triangulation

Teach **Plurality and coordination**: incompatible references may each be
internally coherent; coordination does not require flattening them into one
authority.

### Stage 9: Lumii Threshold and Forecast

Teach the limit of the relationship. Lumii's refusal is a boundary, not a
puzzle prompt. Continuing creates a personal rupture and a defense forecast.
The forecast tests whether Lumii believes physical resistance could preserve
the Vault without unacceptable casualty.

### Stage 10: Vault opening and hub

Teach:

- the Cipher and Lumii's firewall were independent safeguards;
- Blueprints automatically manifest Projects when exact requirements are met;
- Project manifestation and Project effect timing may be separate;
- Antimatter is the first recovered Blueprint, not the complete answer to the
  Vault's danger;
- later records are intentionally corrupted or unavailable at launch.

Story progression ends cleanly here for the initial publication. The First
Charge and Basilisk continuation must not appear as broken or immediately
playable actions.

## 9. Control Tutorial Contract

### 9.1 Required learning outcomes

By completion, a player should be able to:

1. identify the shared Well and personal holdings;
2. perform both legal Harness patterns;
3. read an Artifact cost and Forge it;
4. explain that paid Affinities return when implementation becomes self-sustaining;
5. understand that bonuses are permanent capabilities;
6. explain Encrypt as isolation of an unmastered path;
7. recognize Singularity as finite interface capacity and a special substitute,
   not a natural Affinity or energy from the Architect's universe;
8. identify Eminence and the equal-turn closing objective;
9. understand that an Affinity pattern creates eligibility for Luminary notice
   while the Luminary still chooses;
10. state that the tutorial civilization was simulated while meeting Lumii was real;
11. state that an Architect influences but does not rule civilizations;
12. identify Lumii as a continuing person rather than a menu voice.

### 9.2 Beat-level contract

| Beat family | Mechanical job | Fantasy job | Required inference | Misconception prevented |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contact and location | Establish attention and dialogue controls | Lumii independently locates the player | Someone is reaching across a boundary | Generic narrator or system prompt |
| Naming the Architect | Establish player role | Define limited external influence | I can shape reachable paths without owning people | I am a god-emperor |
| Invitation or refusal | Teach consequential dialogue choice | Establish consent and Lumii's respect for refusal | I may leave without punishment | The tutorial traps me |
| Entry and Affinity reveal | Introduce the visual language | Present five morally neutral modes | Colors express philosophies, not elemental fuel | Five kinds of energy |
| Well and Forge reveal | Orient the board | Translate supply and technological frontier | The UI compresses a larger history | This is only a shop board |
| Harness actions | Teach legal collection | Stabilize temporary alignments | Tokens are commitments I can later direct | I mine colored resources |
| First Forge | Teach cost, confirmation, paid-Affinity return, and permanent bonus | Civilization performs operational mastery across centuries | The card is a readable path; the lasting capability remains after temporary Affinities return | I personally build the card or own the idea |
| Encrypt | Teach private path storage | Isolate present legibility without mastery or a signature | Rivals lose current access, not future discovery | Reserve, steal, or Forge |
| Singularity | Teach current wildcard behavior | Expose finite interface capacity | It is exceptional and belongs to the interface, not either universe's energy supply | Sixth Affinity, Flux, or imported energy |
| Eminence and closure | Teach score and end condition | Define observed consequence | Winning closes an epoch; it does not prove virtue | Score equals morality |
| Luminary demonstration | Teach eligibility and effect relationship | Show compatible contact through the Terminus | A Luminary chooses relation and is not owned | Summoned collectible unit |
| Completion and handoff | Return control to player | Distinguish simulation from first real history | The next match matters differently | Every match is a simulation |

### 9.3 Pacing rules

- Teach one new action or truth at a time.
- Prefer action followed immediately by consequence over paragraphs of advance
  explanation.
- Keep Lumii's important dialogue sentence-by-sentence.
- Never put a required instruction beneath the fold on a static mobile scene.
- Let static cinematic phases advance after a short input guard; consequential
  choices require explicit buttons.
- Preserve sequence and cause/effect in reduced motion; reduce travel, shake,
  spin, flash, and parallax instead of deleting meaning.
- Audio may strengthen recognition but may not carry unique information.
- Make deeper philosophy optional in the Archive after the core concept is
  understood.

The target duration should be calibrated through human testing. Speed is not a
success if players complete the tutorial while misunderstanding the fantasy.

## 10. Story Teaching Contracts

### 10.1 First Contact calibration

The canonical Story Framework currently calls for Curious, Guarded, and
Resolute First Contact stances. They calibrate Lumii's early understanding of
the Architect and are not permanent routes or morality classes.

They are implemented through natural responses:

- direct entry records **Resolute**;
- asking what lies beyond records **Curious**;
- questioning Lumii before following records **Guarded**.

They must:

- appear as natural authored responses rather than visible route labels;
- record relationship evidence without locking campaign access;
- change later acknowledgement or tone, not competitive power;
- be implemented and restored consistently;
- be committed only by the account's single completed First Contact run.

Inquiry depth is a separate record. Asking optional questions records discoveries
without changing stance after entry or creating a morality class. Later events
may recognize what the player heard without testing their recall.

The universe-name response also records a neutral rapport memory: receptive,
probing, or sparring. It may influence later acknowledgement and tone, but never
competitive power, access, or Lume. The sparring response receives a restrained
immediate reaction from Lumii; audio reinforces that reaction but carries no
unique meaning. The origin discovery is awarded only after Lumii's complete
origin explanation, never merely for selecting a question that could lead to it.

### 10.2 Completion reward and discovery state

First Contact is canonical story content and can be completed only once per
registered account. Its first completion grants 10 Lume once, after the guest
completion is claimed by a new or existing account. No Lume is granted to an
unregistered guest.

Optional explanations record stable discoveries: Lumii's origin, Artifact
persistence, and Encryption authority. These discoveries grant no separate
reward and create no post-tutorial quiz. They remain available to the event
system so later narrative encounters can acknowledge what the player learned.
Guests may finish First Contact and retain its pending completion locally.
Account creation or sign-in claims completion before preference
synchronization; a network failure never invalidates the locally completed
encounter.

### 10.3 Chronicle teaching

| Experience | Primary philosophical problem | What the player should learn |
|---|---|---|
| The Trace | Agency | Revealing a viable path can empower; suppressing alternatives can turn guidance into rule |
| The Recurrence | Knowledge | Discovery, custody, disclosure, and containment are distinct responsibilities |
| The Triangulation | Plurality | Coordination can preserve incompatible references rather than erase them |
| Lumii Threshold | Relationship and boundary | Understanding another person does not grant authority to override them |
| Vault opening | Consequence and mystery | A comprehensible device may be dangerous without being the deeper danger |

Each scene must establish who is affected, what is happening now, why the
Architect is being asked to act, and what each choice changes. Abstract language
may deepen a clear conflict; it may not substitute for one.

## 11. Copy And Presentation Rules

### 11.1 Canonical copy constraints

Introductory copy must not claim:

- Affinities are simply "elemental forces" or "the energy behind everything";
- the Architect unilaterally determines what a people becomes or commands
  compliance. Lumii's First Contact paraphrase, "You determine what my people
  reach for, and what we become," is permitted in context only because the
  surrounding exchange establishes reachable possibilities and retained
  civilizational choice;
- ordinary matches are simulations;
- Luminaries are summoned possessions;
- Singularity is a natural Affinity or Flux;
- Eminence measures goodness, advancement, population, or total power;
- Artifacts are the first invention of ordinary prerequisite science;
- Affinity is consumed as fuel when an Artifact is Forged;
- Forging is the instantaneous manufacture of the depicted object;
- Artifact signature interference suppresses related technologies or transfers
  intellectual ownership;
- a Blueprint's manifestation always means immediate activation;
- victory is required for a Chronicle to matter.

Preferred plain-language sequence:

1. name the visible thing;
2. state what the player can do;
3. show the consequence;
4. attach the fiction in one clear sentence;
5. offer deeper meaning through inspection or the Archive.

Use `commit`, `master`, and `implement` for Artifacts. Reserve `manifest` for
Blueprints and Projects. Use `Artifact signature` and `signature interference`;
do not use player-facing `wake` for this doctrine.

### 11.2 Mystery rule

Use withheld information to create curiosity about **why**, **what lies beyond**,
or **what a pattern implies**. Do not withhold:

- the speaker;
- the immediate action;
- whether a choice is consequential;
- the local stakes;
- the distinction between simulation and history;
- whether the player can return or withdraw.

A transmission fault may withhold an answer only when the failure itself is
intelligible. It distorts the surrounding interface and never creates another
Lumii. At the border it interrupts `I am an—`, after which Lumii says the answer
will not cross intact and asks the player to inquire again after entry. At the
Vault it may show only `BA······`, `··SILI··`, and `·····ISK`, followed by "Not
a creature" and "A pattern I can describe only by its effects." The interface
never assembles the word or gives a complete explanatory model.

Reduced motion replaces flashing or abrupt distortion with a restrained,
non-flashing interruption. Muted play preserves the full meaning visually, and
all completion callbacks require an idempotent guard and fallback timer.

### 11.3 Visual and audio rule

- The player should see the object or consequence being discussed.
- Affinity titles and philosophical subtitles must remain readable against
  Lumii's core and all motion states.
- Mobile uses a purpose-built composition, not a shrunken desktop frame.
- Required text must stay on screen long enough to read and wait when player
  action is required.
- Sound identifies action, mood, and consequence but never replaces labels,
  captions, or visible state.
- Premium spectacle is concentrated on meaningful transitions: entry,
  manifestation, Luminary relation, Chronicle consequence, Threshold rupture,
  and Vault opening.

## 12. Replayable Reference

The guided tutorial should remain concise because the Archive provides a
persistent reference. The Archive field guide must cover:

- Architect agency and limits;
- the five Affinities and all dyads;
- Well, Harness, Forge, Encrypt, Singularity, Eminence, and closure;
- Artifact discovery, implementation, Damage, and Annihilation;
- Civilization portrait, deployment sites, historical imprint, current
  capability, Stability, Conditions, Maturity, and Reach;
- Luminary personhood and alliance;
- Chronicle primary history versus Rehearsal;
- Blueprints, Project manifestation, and trigger/use distinctions;
- Civilization Outcome, records, historical-quality Lume, and wallet balance.

Players must be able to replay the control tutorial from the Archive or rules
area. Developer-only scene navigation may support review but must never ship as
player progression.

## 13. Comprehension And Validation

Code correctness cannot prove that onboarding works. Release approval requires
observed first-time-player evidence.

### 13.1 Mechanical checks

Without coaching after the relevant lesson, the player can:

- identify at least one legal Harness action;
- read and pay a Forge cost;
- locate permanent bonuses and Eminence;
- explain what Encrypt changes;
- recognize the closing objective;
- find the Civilization portrait and one causal dossier.

### 13.2 Fantasy checks

Ask in the player's own words:

1. Who is Lumii?
2. What is an Architect able to do?
3. Does the Architect control the people in a civilization?
4. What are Affinities: literal energy, philosophies, colors, or something
   else?
5. Was the tutorial civilization real?
6. Are ordinary matches and Chronicles real within the fiction?
7. What does Forging an Artifact mean for the civilization?
8. Does a Luminary belong to the player?
9. What does Eminence measure?
10. Where can the player revisit these explanations?

The launch target is at least 80 percent unaided comprehension for each core
truth across the required external-player cohort, with no single misconception
shared by more than two participants. Any repeated misunderstanding triggers a
copy, staging, or presentation revision and a fresh test.

### 13.3 Experience checks

Observe:

- where players hesitate without curiosity;
- where they advance before reading;
- where they confuse decorative animation with an actionable control;
- where terminology appears before its referent;
- whether mystery feels intriguing or merely unclear;
- whether mobile composition causes scrolling during static decisions;
- whether muted and reduced-motion play preserve every required inference;
- whether the transition from simulation to first real history feels
  significant.

## 16. Change Control

Exact dialogue authorship and approval state are governed by
`LUMINAe_DIALOGUE_AUTHORSHIP_CONTRACT_v1.0.md`. Broad approval to improve the
tutorial, fix its interface, or optimize onboarding does not authorize changes
to locked or approved wording. Runtime copy absent from the typed authorship
ledger remains draft even when it is already visible in the product.

Any change to introductory copy or sequencing must answer four questions:

1. What mechanical behavior does this line or beat teach?
2. What player-fantasy truth does it establish?
3. What misconception does it prevent?
4. Where can the player revisit it later?

If a line answers none of them and does not carry necessary character or mood,
it should be cut. If a line creates a contradiction, spectacle does not rescue
it.

When canon changes:

1. update the authoritative source;
2. update this doctrine if the onboarding contract changes;
3. update runtime copy and reference material together;
4. update automated beat expectations;
5. rerun human comprehension for any changed core truth.
