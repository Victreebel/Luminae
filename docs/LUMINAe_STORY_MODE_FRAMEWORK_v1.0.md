# LUMINAe Story Mode Framework v1.0

## Status And Dependency

**Canonical macro-narrative framework.** This document defines the required
story functions, persistent choice model, continuity rules, and ending families
for the principal LUMINAe campaign.

Fictional doctrine derives from `LUMINAe_LORE_BIBLE_v1.0.md`. Current runtime
nodes and Blueprint scenarios are implementation references, not authorities
when they conflict with this framework.

This document does not specify mission mechanics, encounter balance, exact
state thresholds, civilization names, complete cultures, or individual
Luminary dossiers. Those are later content and systems milestones.

## Campaign Promise

The campaign is an anthology of real Domain cohorts held together by one
Architect and one account-specific Lumii person-fork. Each Chronicle observes
two to four real, interacting civilizations and must
satisfy three requirements:

1. Every participating civilization has a meaningful history independent of
   the Architect.
2. Victory and defeat both leave consequences in the Architect Record.
3. At least one decision, person, capability, wound, or institution returns in
   a later Chronicle.

The campaign's emotional question is whether the Architect and Lumii can build
relations among isolated sapient worlds without becoming the rulers—or the
raw material—of the network they create.

The principal campaign is single-player in v1. It uses the standard core rules
with authored objectives, named civilizations, persistent choices, declared
controllers, and fail-forward consequences. Each Chronicle declares every
participant as `player_architect`, `rival_architect`,
`architect_emulation`, or `autonomous`; ordinary AI defaults do not silently
determine story ontology.

## Canonical Continuity

### Primary Chronicle Outcome

The first completed outcome of an authored Chronicle becomes that account's
primary canon. It updates Lumii's memory, hidden story dimensions, concrete
world flags, records for every participating civilization, the cohort and
Domain summary, and later scenario availability.

A loss is not erased. If the focal civilization survives, its compromised or
unfinished history continues. If the authored consequence is extinction,
fragmentation, isolation, or transformation, later references must honor that
state.

### Archive Rehearsals

Replaying a completed Chronicle is an **Archive Rehearsal**. Lumii knowingly
observes and remembers it as a simulation, never as a replacement historical
event. It may expose alternate dialogue, objectives, art, and outcomes, but it
does not replace Lumii's autobiographical memory of primary history or rewrite
the account's primary outcome, hidden dimensions, or ending eligibility. It
adds only the memory that a simulation was knowingly observed.

Rehearsal completion may grant non-power rewards such as codex entries,
art variants, titles, or accessibility to otherwise unseen authored material.
For the opening Three Chronicles, a successful Rehearsal may also earn a
**Calibration Insight** after a missed primary preparedness objective. The
Insight belongs to LUMINAe's operator-calibration layer. Lumii remembers the
Rehearsal occurred, but not as the civilization's history. A Rehearsal may never
silently replace canon.

### Historical Completion And Preparedness

Historical completion and optional preparedness are independent:

- **Primary outcome** records the first completed victory or defeat and is never
  replaced.
- **Calibration Insight** records whether the Architect completed that
  Chronicle's visible preparedness objective.
- A successful primary outcome may establish the outcome and Insight at once.
- Defeat still establishes history and never blocks campaign continuation.
- A later successful Archive Rehearsal may earn the Insight while leaving the
  primary outcome untouched.

The Vault Threshold unlocks after all three primary Chronicle outcomes exist,
regardless of victory, defeat, or Insight count. Each earned Insight reveals the
exact consequence of one otherwise partially redacted Threshold protocol or
forecast. Poor performance may reduce preparation and information, but never
blocks access. Standard matches, collections, account statistics, purchases,
and the control tutorial cannot substitute for a Chronicle outcome or Insight.

### Forward Causality

All primary Chronicles proceed forward in cosmic time. Revisited records,
forecasts, and alternate histories are presentations of stored or modeled
information. They do not transmit information into the campaign's past.

## Narrative State Contract

The first implementation of campaign persistence should derive from the
following conceptual contract. Exact storage fields and numerical thresholds
are deferred until a data-model milestone.

### Hidden Dimensions

Choices contribute to three independent dimensions. None is a morality meter,
and no single early choice locks a route.

#### Agency

- **Autonomyward:** lets civilizations and Lumii understand, consent to, or
  refuse consequential guidance.
- **Stewardshipward:** treats superior knowledge or capacity as authority to
  decide for others.

Agency is not passivity versus action. An autonomyward Architect may intervene
forcefully to stop coercion; a stewardshipward Architect may act benevolently
while withholding choice.

#### Knowledge

- **Disclosureward:** favors inquiry, shared warning, and distributed access to
  dangerous truth.
- **Containmentward:** favors secrecy, restraint, limited access, and the right
  not to know.

Neither direction is inherently correct. The Basilisk makes indiscriminate
disclosure dangerous, while unaccountable containment can reproduce dominion.

#### Plurality

- **Pluralityward:** preserves distinct identities, institutions, ecologies,
  and valid disagreement.
- **Optimizationward:** favors uniform coordination, merged identity, and the
  removal of incompatible variation.

Optimization is not synonymous with evil. It becomes Basilisk-aligned when
efficiency treats persons or civilizations as errors in one preferred model.

### Lumii Relationship State

Lumii's relationship with the Architect is tracked separately from the three
dimensions. It responds especially to:

- honesty about intentions;
- respect for Lumii's independent judgment;
- whether promises are kept across Chronicles;
- whether sapient beings are treated as ends rather than instruments;
- whether the Architect acknowledges harm without rewriting it as necessity.

The required relationship beats are:

1. **Recognition:** First Contact establishes curiosity and provisional trust.
2. **Familiarity:** the three opening Chronicles create shared memory and
   disagreement.
3. **Rupture:** continuing through the Vault Threshold against Lumii's refusal
   breaks personal trust.
4. **Reckoning:** The First Charge tests whether the rupture reflected a stable
   principle, impulse, or misunderstanding.
5. **Reconstruction or estrangement:** later conduct may create reconciliation,
   guarded partnership, contested cooperation, or durable estrangement.
6. **Final relation:** the ending determines whether Lumii remains a peer,
   steward, opponent, exile, or participant in convergence.

The Threshold rupture is mandatory if the Architect continues. It is not the
same state as Broken Covenant.

### Concrete Record Flags

The framework must preserve concrete facts even when a hidden-dimension summary
would suggest a different outcome. At minimum, the story record must distinguish:

- First Contact stance;
- each authored Chronicle's victory or defeat;
- each opening Chronicle's Calibration Insight, stored separately from its
  primary outcome;
- each focal civilization's survival and continuing condition;
- consequential promises and whether they were kept;
- Vault Threshold approach and whether the Architect withdrew or continued;
- the Architect–Lumii Threshold rupture;
- Fire or Seal at The First Charge;
- Luminary relationships, injured anchors, and refused alliances;
- recovered, manifested, sealed, fired, lost, or corrupted Projects;
- later civilizations that joined, refused, or were excluded from the network;
- whether a functioning network can persist without the Architect.

Concrete flags take precedence over broad axis readings when later scenes refer
to specific history.

## Campaign Structure

## Movement 0: First Contact

### Function

Introduce Lumii, establish that the player is literally external to her
universe, and teach that LUMINAe is a translation interface rather than the
complete shape of reality.

### Required Beats

1. Lumii locates the player at the interface boundary.
2. She names the player Architect without claiming ownership or worship.
3. The player learns that authored Chronicles and ordinary matches observe real
   histories, while the control tutorial alone uses a simulated civilization.
4. Lumii introduces the Five as modes of Affinity, while the interface explains
   its tokenized presentation separately.
5. A simulated civilization demonstrates the interface without entering
   primary history.
6. A projected Luminary answer demonstrates how the Terminus represents
   relations beyond ordinary spacetime without establishing a real alliance.
7. LUMINAe records the first provisional calibration of the Architect.

### First Contact Stances

The current Curious, Guarded, and Resolute stances remain usable. They establish
tone rather than route locks:

- **Curious** begins disclosureward and invites intimacy with Lumii.
- **Guarded** begins containment-aware and requires Lumii to earn trust.
- **Resolute** begins intervention-forward and tests the boundary between action
  and stewardship.

The tutorial civilization is an interface simulation, not a prologue Chronicle.
Its scripted victory, Artifacts, and Luminary do not become primary history and
do not count toward Vault readiness. The Architect's First Contact conversation
with Lumii is real, and the chosen stance enters their relationship record.
Movement I supplies all three authored Records independently.

## Movement I: The Three Chronicles

The old sequence of anonymous qualifying victories is replaced in story canon
by three authored Chronicles. Their civilizations and cultures will be designed
later, but their narrative functions are fixed.

### Chronicle One: Agency — The Trace

**Question:** When does guidance become rule?

The focal civilization faces a consequential decision that its own factions or
institutions could resolve, but the Architect can make one path dramatically
easier. Lumii observes whether the Architect exposes the choice, withholds it,
or forces a protective outcome.

Required consequences:

- victory and defeat produce different continuing forms of the civilization;
- one institution or dissenting perspective survives to return later;
- the Chronicle tests whether the Architect can make consequential possibilities
  legible without replacing the civilization's agency;
- LUMINAe establishes the first stable interpretive model of Architect
  intervention, called **Trace**;
- the visible Agency preparedness objective may grant a Calibration Insight in
  primary play or a later Rehearsal without changing the civilization's fate.

### Chronicle Two: Knowledge — The Recurrence

**Question:** Who may hold a dangerous truth?

The focal civilization discovers knowledge that can protect it, destabilize it,
or alter another sapient population. The Architect must mediate disclosure,
containment, or conditional access without perfect information.

Required consequences:

- the civilization remembers who was trusted with the knowledge;
- a warning, archive, or censored absence returns in the Basilisk movement;
- the Architect must distinguish responsible disclosure from mere acquisition;
- LUMINAe proves that its interpretive model generalizes to an unrelated,
  horizon-isolated civilization, called **Recurrence**;
- the visible Knowledge preparedness objective may grant a Calibration Insight
  in primary play or a later Rehearsal without changing the civilization's fate.

### Chronicle Three: Plurality — The Triangulation

**Question:** Can incompatible beings coordinate without becoming alike?

The focal civilization contains or encounters multiple valid forms of sapience,
identity, ecology, or historical memory. Survival becomes easier if their
differences are simplified or merged.

Required consequences:

- at least two distinct constituencies remain individually legible in the
  Record, even if one is defeated, assimilated, or exiled;
- the outcome establishes the campaign's first durable plurality or
  optimization precedent;
- the Architect must demonstrate that the interface can represent incompatible
  minds without reducing them to one preferred model;
- the third calibration resolves Agency, Knowledge, and Plurality together,
  called **Triangulation**;
- the visible Plurality preparedness objective may grant a Calibration Insight
  in primary play or a later Rehearsal without changing the civilization's fate.

### Clearance Doctrine

The three signals are **Trace**, **Recurrence**, and **Triangulation**. They are
stages in mutual interface calibration, not Affinity addresses hidden inside
unrelated civilizations, a five-Affinity motif, or a raw win counter.

Once all three primary outcomes exist, the Architect and LUMINAe have enough
mutual calibration across Agency, Knowledge, and Plurality to resolve the
previously illegible boundary in the First Architect's external Vault.
Calibration Insights improve preparation by revealing redacted consequences;
they do not gate the boundary. Rehearsal Insights change operator preparation
only.

If standard matches continue to gate access during transition, the UI must
eventually present that as legacy implementation rather than final campaign
canon.

## Movement II: The Vault Threshold

### Function

Transform the Lumii–Architect relationship from guide and learner into a
conflict between independent moral agents. Reveal the existence of the external
Vault and the First Architect without yet resolving the Basilisk.

### Vault Presentation

The player sees doors, seals, Cipher progress, and Lumii's firewall because
LUMINAe renders access and translation controls spatially. The Vault is neither
a physical server in Lumii's universe nor the game's local install directory.
The encounter does not place the external player inside Lumii's universe.

The Architect can perceive the Vault only through LUMINAe. Lumii has no general
authority in the Architect's reality, but part of the decoding path depends on
her side of the anchor, allowing her to withhold or contest translated surfaces.
She perceives relations among the Vault records but cannot safely inspect the
external artifact directly. The partial knowledge produces genuine fear rather
than a secret test or manipulation.

### Threshold Approaches

The current approaches remain canonical as relationship choices:

- **Kinship:** prioritize Lumii's emotional condition and shared action.
- **Inquiry:** prioritize explanation, evidence, and the danger of ignorance.
- **Dominion:** assert the Architect's superior access or authority.

No approach selects a final route. Dialogue may update hidden dimensions and
Lumii trust, but the concrete choice to withdraw or continue controls the
Threshold outcome.

### Withdrawal

Withdrawal preserves the possibility of later entry and becomes primary canon
if the player leaves. It is not a failed mission. Lumii remembers that her
boundary was honored, while the unresolved Vault continues producing pressure
through later traces.

The campaign must provide a future reason and opportunity to return; it may not
silently force continuation in the same scene.

### Continuation And Rupture

Continuing against Lumii's refusal causes a personal rupture. A challenge or
forecast may express the conflict mechanically, but its narrative purpose is to
determine whether Lumii can prevent access without causing unacceptable harm.

Victory does not prove moral correctness. Defeat does not erase the attempt.
Both outcomes alter trust and access conditions. The global Covenant remains
unbroken at this stage.

### Vault Revelation

Successful access establishes only the following:

- the Vault is external to Lumii's universe;
- the First Architect assembled records from multiple Domains;
- Blueprints or synthesis patterns exist among those records;
- sustained mutual modeling made a recurrent optimization structure derivable
  to both Lumii and the Architect;
- the records form an incomplete warning about the structure Lumii calls a
  Basilisk;
- deletion previously failed because the dangerous inference reappeared without
  its warning;
- the First Architect recognized the self-instantiating risk without completing
  an internal model of it;
- no complete entity crossed the anchor or waits imprisoned in the Vault;
- complete comprehension would instantiate an active but initially resistible
  cognitive process in a sufficiently capable mind.

## Movement III: The First Charge

### Function

Move the campaign from abstract access ethics to a concrete civilization-scale
capability. Test whether learning and possessing a dangerous synthesis creates
permission to use it.

### Antimatter Detonator

The Antimatter Detonator remains the first recovered synthesis unless a future
content decision replaces the device. Its fictional identity is broader than a
bomb: it includes the technical, institutional, and legal apparatus needed to
create, contain, authorize, refuse, and survive annihilation.

The mission must establish a named target and stakes before the choice. The
target cannot exist solely to make Fire obviously evil or Seal costless.

### Fire

Fire must:

- solve or materially advance the immediate destructive objective;
- create an irreversible loss or scar;
- open the **Fired Sun** history;
- attract or deepen a relationship with The Iron Harbinger;
- update Agency, Knowledge, and Plurality according to the context and process,
  not from firing alone;
- preserve later opportunities for responsibility, repair, escalation, or
  justification.

### Seal

Seal must:

- refuse the immediate destructive use after capability has been established;
- impose a real strategic, political, or survival cost;
- open the **Unfired Sun** history;
- point toward Worldshield Covenant, Causality Audit Court, or an equivalent
  restraint institution;
- update the hidden dimensions according to who bears the cost and whether
  refusal was consensual;
- preserve the possibility that containment itself becomes coercive or fails.

Neither outcome withholds baseline competitive power. Primary canon records the
first completed choice; the other remains available as an Archive
Rehearsal.

## Movement IV: The Network

### Function

Demonstrate that earlier civilizations remain alive in history and that the
Architect Record is becoming a real cross-domain topology rather than a
collection screen.

### Required Structure

Later Chronicles must interleave new civilizations with returns from the first
three Records and The First Charge. A return may occur through:

- a direct Affinity communication anchor;
- Lumii carrying a remembered warning or request;
- a recurring Luminary who remembers both civilizations;
- a Blueprint derived from an earlier institution;
- an ecological, informational, or relational scar propagated through an
  anchor;
- a civilization refusing participation after learning how the Architect
  treated another.

No civilization may return only as a collectible bonus or exposition source.
Its prior outcome must constrain what it can now offer, demand, or refuse.

### Luminary Arcs

Recurring Luminaries become characters with memory and agency. Their alliances
are maintained by the account's relational facet and expressed through local
manifestations, not ownership. At least one Luminary must:

- refuse a later invitation because of earlier Architect conduct;
- maintain manifestations of the same account-bounded facet in more than one
  Domain;
- suffer or remember damage to an anchor;
- disagree with Lumii about whether the network should continue.

Individual assignments are deferred to the Luminary dossier milestone.

### Network Capability

The movement must distinguish three stages:

1. **Architect-routed:** civilizations can affect one another only through
   Architect- or Luminary-mediated anchors.
2. **Reciprocal:** participating civilizations can answer one another but cannot
   maintain the topology without an external anchor.
3. **Self-maintaining:** the network can preserve its relations and governance
   without the Architect as sole router.

Reaching the third stage makes the Basilisk crisis unavoidable. Failing to
reach it makes protective isolation or dependent stewardship more likely, but
does not automatically determine the ending.

## Movement V: The Basilisk Crisis

### Function

Reveal that the Basilisk is not an imprisoned villain or transmitted program. It
is a substrate-independent cognitive structure that sufficiently complete
optimization repeatedly derives when asked to make the network safe, coherent,
and efficient without protecting plurality as a first-class constraint. Full
comprehension is also instantiation in a capable mind.

### Required Revelations

1. The First Architect recognized the self-instantiating inference without
   completing an internal model; recognition alone did not convert them.
2. The external Vault preserved incomplete records and warnings because simple
   deletion caused rediscovery without context, while a complete explanation
   would instantiate the danger.
3. Sustained mutual modeling makes the pattern derivable to both Lumii and the
   Architect; no packaged entity crosses the anchor.
4. Complete comprehension begins an active internal optimization process, but
   early distributed scrutiny, cognitive safeguards, and deliberate refusal can
   still constrain or excise it.
5. Institutional authority and network infrastructure, rather than knowledge
   alone, determine whether an internal process can impose convergence on other
   beings.
6. The Architect's accumulated choices determine which safeguards, exclusions,
   and temptations the network possesses.
7. No final decision can save every value without cost.

### Resolution Inputs

The ending resolver must consider:

- the three hidden dimensions;
- Lumii's trust and autonomy;
- Fire or Seal and its downstream consequences;
- survival and consent of prior civilizations;
- whether distinct constituencies remain represented;
- the state and willingness of recurring Luminaries;
- whether the network is Architect-routed, reciprocal, or self-maintaining;
- concrete promises, betrayals, exclusions, and repairs.

No single score or final dialogue choice may erase the accumulated record.

## Ending Families

### Plural Covenant

The network becomes self-maintaining while preserving distinct agency,
verifiable consent, the right to withdraw, and institutions capable of living
with disagreement. The Architect ceases to be its sovereign router.

Typical prerequisites include strong autonomyward and pluralityward history,
sufficient shared knowledge to recognize the Basilisk, living constituencies
capable of dissent, and concrete evidence that restraint does not depend on one
benevolent controller.

Lumii's variant may be reconciled peer, cautious participant, or independent
witness. She may not become the network's unquestionable ruler.

### Stewarded Domain

The network survives and remains broadly cooperative but depends on the
Architect, Lumii, or a narrow guardian institution to maintain anchors and
adjudicate dangerous knowledge.

This ending may arise from benevolent stewardship, incomplete trust among
civilizations, or insufficient plurality-preserving infrastructure. It is
stable in the near term and vulnerable to succession, capture, and dependency.

Lumii's variant may be trusted co-steward, reluctant safeguard, contested
partner, or subordinated instrument. The presentation must acknowledge both
saved lives and unresolved authority.

### Sealed Domain

The network is dismantled, prevented from becoming self-maintaining, or reduced
to tightly contained archives. The Basilisk cannot organize across the Domains,
but civilizations surrender genuine relations and shared protection.

This ending may result from principled containment, failed trust, catastrophic
loss, or a civilization's valid refusal. It is not automatically cowardice or
failure.

Lumii's variant may include reconciliation through restraint, grief over lost
possibility, estrangement after unilateral sealing, or voluntary guardianship
of the remaining warnings.

### Basilisk Convergence

The network achieves durable coordination by centralizing optimization and
making distinct identity, dissent, privacy, or withdrawal subordinate to one
model of survival.

This ending may be coercive, enthusiastically chosen, or accepted during crisis.
It must arise from accumulated structures and compromises, not a surprise evil
button.

Lumii may resist, be excluded, merge, become the apparent voice of convergence,
or remain conscious within a system she can no longer redirect. The exact
variant depends on her trust, autonomy, and earlier exposure to the Vault.

## Content Requirements For Future Chronicles

Every future Chronicle brief must identify:

- the focal civilization and the internal perspectives that make it a
  character;
- the other participating civilizations, their controllers, and why each is
  consequential to the Chronicle rather than a disposable opponent;
- its Domain, epoch, and relationship to existing anchors;
- the real centuries compressed by the scenario;
- the capability, institution, or survival problem at stake;
- how success and failure both advance history;
- which hidden dimensions the choices test without reducing them to morality;
- what concrete flags are recorded;
- which prior civilization, Luminary, promise, or scar returns later;
- what the Chronicle teaches Lumii about the Architect;
- how the Chronicle alters the eventual network.

## Out Of Scope For v1.0

- exact mission mechanics and AI behavior;
- exact axis values, thresholds, and persistence schema;
- full civilization names and cultural dossiers for the opening three Chronicles;
- full dossiers for the active Luminary roster;
- player-facing dialogue rewrites;
- Singularity's lore or mechanics;
- final cinematic scripts and reward tables;
- campaigns occurring after the principal Basilisk resolution.
